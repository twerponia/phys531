π⁰ → γγ decay and invariant mass reconstruction

This note explains the Python code that simulates a neutral pion (π⁰) decaying into two photons and then reconstructs the parent invariant mass from the measured photon energies and directions. The key physics idea is that the invariant mass is the same in all inertial frames.

1. The decay kinematics: photon_pair(M, E)

def photon_pair(M, E):
    g = E/M;  b = np.sqrt(1 - 1/g**2);  k = M/2
    boost = lambda p: np.array([g*(p[0] + b*p[3]), p[1], p[2], g*(p[3] + b*p[0])])
    return boost(np.array([k,  k, 0., 0.])), boost(np.array([k, -k, 0., 0.]))

1.1 Physical setup

We consider a neutral particle (π⁰) of mass $M$ moving along the $+z$ direction with lab energy $E$. Its Lorentz factor and speed are

$$ \gamma = \frac{E}{M}, \qquad \beta = \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{\gamma^2}}. $$

In the π⁰ rest frame, the decay $\pi^0 \to \gamma_1 + \gamma_2$ is symmetric: each photon has energy

$$ k = \frac{M}{2}, $$

and momentum magnitude $|\vec p| = k$ (since for photons $E = |\vec p|$ in units with $c=1$). The code chooses the two photons in the rest frame as

  • Photon 1: four-vector $(E, p_x, p_y, p_z) = (k, k, 0, 0)$ (moving along $+x$)
  • Photon 2: four-vector $(k, -k, 0, 0)$ (moving along $-x$)

1.2 The Lorentz boost

The function boost applies a Lorentz boost along the $+z$ direction to a four-vector $p = (E, p_x, p_y, p_z)$:

$$ \begin{aligned} E' &= \gamma \left(E + \beta p_z\right), \\ p_z' &= \gamma \left(p_z + \beta E\right), \\ p_x' &= p_x, \\ p_y' &= p_y. \end{aligned} $$

This is implemented as: 

boost = lambda p: np.array([g*(p[0] + b*p[3]), p[1], p[2], g*(p[3] + b*p[0])])

The function photon_pair returns the two photon four-momenta in the lab frame: 

return boost(np.array([k,  k, 0., 0.])), boost(np.array([k, -k, 0., 0.]))

So the decay is defined in the π⁰ rest frame and then boosted to the lab frame where the parent has energy $E$.

2. Reconstructing the invariant mass: M_naive(p1, p2)

def M_naive(p1, p2):
    E1, E2 = p1[0], p2[0]
    v1, v2 = p1[1:], p2[1:]
    cos_th = np.dot(v1, v2)/(np.linalg.norm(v1)*np.linalg.norm(v2))
    return np.sqrt(max(2*E1*E2*(1 - cos_th), 0.0))

2.1 Invariant mass definition

For any system with total energy $E_{\text{tot}}$ and total momentum $\vec p_{\text{tot}}$, the invariant mass $M$ is defined by

$$ M^2 c^4 = E_{\text{tot}}^2 - |\vec p_{\text{tot}}|^2 c^2. $$

For two photons with energies $E_1, E_2$ and angle $\theta$ between their momenta, one can show (using four-vectors) that the invariant mass of the two-photon system is

$$ M^2 c^4 = 2 E_1 E_2 (1 - \cos\theta). $$

In units with $c = 1$, this becomes

$$ M^2 = 2 E_1 E_2 (1 - \cos\theta), \qquad M = \sqrt{2 E_1 E_2 (1 - \cos\theta)}. $$

2.2 What the code does

The function M_naive implements exactly this formula:

  • Extract photon energies: $$E_1 = p1[0], \quad E_2 = p2[0].$$
  • Extract photon 3-momenta: $$\vec v_1 = p1[1:], \quad \vec v_2 = p2[1:].$$
  • Compute the angle between the photon directions: $$ \cos\theta = \frac{\vec v_1 \cdot \vec v_2}{|\vec v_1|\,|\vec v_2|}. $$
  • Apply the invariant mass formula: $$ M_{\text{reco}} = \sqrt{2 E_1 E_2 (1 - \cos\theta)}. $$

The max(..., 0.0) inside the square root is a numerical safeguard: floating-point roundoff can occasionally make the argument slightly negative (e.g. $-10^{-15}$), and taking the square root of a negative number would produce an error. Clipping at zero avoids that.

3. Testing Lorentz invariance

M_PI = 0.1349768                      # GeV, the true pi0 mass
for e in range(1, 8):
    E = M_PI*10.0**e
    p1, p2 = photon_pair(M_PI, E)
    print(f"E = {E:9.3e} GeV    M_reco = {M_naive(p1, p2):20.15f}")

Here, M_PI is the physical π⁰ mass in GeV. The loop chooses increasingly large lab energies:

$$ E = 10 M_\pi,\; 100 M_\pi,\; 1000 M_\pi,\; \dots,\; 10^7 M_\pi. $$

For each energy:

  1. photon_pair(M_PI, E) generates the two photon four-momenta in the lab frame.
  2. M_naive(p1, p2) reconstructs the invariant mass from the photon energies and directions.
  3. The code prints the lab energy $E$ and the reconstructed mass $M_{\text{reco}}$.

4. Summary

  • photon_pair constructs a symmetric $\pi^0 \to \gamma\gamma$ decay in the rest frame and boosts it to the lab.
  • M_naive uses the two-photon invariant mass formula $$ M^2 = 2 E_1 E_2 (1 - \cos\theta) $$ to reconstruct the parent mass from measured photon energies and directions.
  • The loop over increasing $E$ shows that $M_{\text{reco}}$ remains equal to $M_\pi$, illustrating that invariant mass is frame-independent.
